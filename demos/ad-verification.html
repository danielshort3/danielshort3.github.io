    How this demo works

    Reported is not the same as attributed. The fictional ad platform reports exposure, website analytics reports an optional session, and a location partner reports a visit. An attribution service then checks the earlier ad and visit records against an example 30-day rule. The resulting signed decision references those exact records. A visit outside the window is recorded but not credited. A website visit is not required.

    People overlap; records are appended. Five independent traveler lanes share one clock and one ordered block writer. A recorded check appears only when its real cryptographic verification passes and the block is appended. New arrivals replace individual completed travelers, never groups. The selected mix applies only to new arrivals.

    Inspect the evidence. Select a block to pause and expand its explanation. Supporting-record buttons open the earlier evidence. Fingerprints and the full signed record stay under Technical details. Editing changes a separate copy, not the authentic ledger; rechecking detects a mismatch and keeps the original totals intact.

    Real cryptography, local simulation. SHA-256, ECDSA signatures, previous-block links and three local validator approvals verify recorded bytes against the session’s original checkpoint. All signers run in this browser—not independent organizations or distributed consensus. No real travelers, device IDs, GPS, or advertising integrations are connected. Example occurrence days and accelerated receipt timing are illustrative, not certified timestamps or real conversion rates.

    Missing observations are not proof of no trip. The demo uses one event per block for clarity; a block can contain multiple events. Reload/reset discards the local session. Before 256 blocks, new arrivals stop early enough for current travelers to finish. The unlisted URL is public to anyone who has it.
